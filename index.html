🎬 Sinners (2025)
9.1
Horror / Drama 2025 137 min

Sinners

Directed by Ryan Coogler

★★★★★

Ryan Coogler's Sinners is a rare beast — a film that wears its ambitions openly and somehow exceeds every one of them. Set in 1930s Mississippi, the story of twin brothers returning home to open a juke joint is wrapped in the language of blues mythology, vampiric folklore, and searing social history. Michael B. Jordan delivers a double performance of remarkable range, and the film's jaw-dropping centrepiece musical sequence stands as one of the great set pieces in recent cinema. Violent, funny, heartbreaking, and alive with ancestral spirit, this is filmmaking that genuinely matters.

Who We Are

About Daily Dose Movies

Daily Dose Movies is an independent film review website dedicated to serious, original, and accessible film criticism. We believe cinema is one of the most powerful art forms in human history — and that every film, big or small, deserves honest, thoughtful engagement.

We started this site because we were tired of reading reviews that felt like marketing copy, or endless numbered lists with no real analysis. We wanted a place where someone genuinely passionate about film could write about what they watched and why it mattered.

What We Cover

We cover everything — new releases in cinemas, streaming originals, international films, classic retrospectives, and overlooked hidden gems. No genre bias. No studio allegiances. If it's a film worth discussing, we'll discuss it.

How We Rate Films

Our ratings run from 1 to 10 and are accompanied by full written reviews of at least 500 words. A 5 is genuinely average, not a disaster. A 10 is genuinely rare. We don't give 8s out of politeness. Every score is earned.

  • 9–10: Essential cinema. Watch it immediately.
  • 7–8: Highly recommended with minor reservations.
  • 5–6: Worth watching for specific audiences.
  • 3–4: Flawed but occasionally interesting.
  • 1–2: Genuinely poor filmmaking.

Who Writes Here

Daily Dose Movies is run by a small team of film lovers who have been watching, discussing, and writing about cinema for over a decade. We have no professional obligation to be kind to studios, no advertising relationships that influence our reviews, and no interest in clickbait.

We just love movies — and we hope you do too.
